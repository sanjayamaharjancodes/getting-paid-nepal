Payoneer in Nepal: Complete Setup Guide

Payoneer is the payout method most Nepali freelancers on Upwork and Fiverr end up using — it's built to receive freelance-platform payouts and international client payments, and it withdraws directly to a Nepali bank account. This guide walks through the setup at a level of detail you can actually act on, and is honest about what we haven't independently verified.

What Payoneer actually is

Payoneer is a global payments company that gives freelancers and small businesses a way to receive money from clients and platforms abroad, then move it to a local bank account. It's widely used across South Asia specifically because it handles a problem PayPal and Stripe don't solve here: getting USD (or other major currencies) out of a platform like Upwork or a direct client, and into a Nepali bank, through a route Nepal's banking system recognizes.

Practically, when you sign up, you get a Payoneer account with receiving details in major currencies (commonly USD, EUR, GBP among others). Clients or platforms pay into those receiving details as if they were paying a local account in that currency; Payoneer then lets you withdraw the balance to your own bank account, or in some cases spend it via a Payoneer card.

Why it's the default for Upwork/Fiverr freelancers here

Upwork and Fiverr both list Payoneer as a supported payout method for Nepal, and it's the most commonly used option among Nepali freelancers on those platforms — a large share of the "how Nepali freelancers get paid" conversation on forums and in freelancer communities is effectively "sign up with Upwork/Fiverr, connect Payoneer, withdraw to your bank." Setting up Payoneer as a Nepal resident

The exact screens Payoneer shows change over time, so treat this as a map of the stages rather than a click-by-click script — always follow what's actually on screen when you sign up.

  1. Create your account. Sign up with your legal name (matching your citizenship document / passport), a working email, and phone number. Payoneer is used by individuals as well as registered businesses — for most freelancers starting out, the individual account type is the relevant one.
  2. Identity verification (KYC). Like any financial platform, Payoneer requires identity verification before you can withdraw funds — typically a government-issued ID (citizenship certificate or passport) and sometimes a proof-of-address step. Have a clear photo or scan ready. This step can take some time to process; don't wait until you're expecting a payment to start it.
  3. Link your Nepali bank account. You'll add your Nepali bank account details (bank name, account number, and the details your bank requires for receiving a Payoneer withdrawal) so Payoneer knows where to send funds when you withdraw. Double-check the account details carefully — a mismatched name between your Payoneer account and your bank account is a common cause of delayed or failed withdrawals.
  4. Connect Payoneer to Upwork/Fiverr (if that's your use case). Inside your Upwork or Fiverr account settings, add Payoneer as your withdrawal/payout method and follow the platform's linking flow, which typically confirms the connection directly with Payoneer.
  5. Receive and withdraw. Once a client or platform pays into your Payoneer balance, you can withdraw to your linked Nepali bank account. Processing time and fees vary — check the current numbers inside your own Payoneer account before relying on a specific figure, since these are exactly the kind of detail that changes without much notice.
On fees and minimum withdrawal amounts: we're deliberately not quoting a specific withdrawal fee percentage or minimum amount here, because Payoneer's published fees can change and we don't want to leave a stale number on this page that misleads you. Check the current fee schedule inside your own Payoneer account or on Payoneer's official fee page before you rely on a number, especially if you're comparing Payoneer against a bank wire for a specific payment size.

PAN and tax — a quick flag, not the full picture

Receiving money through Payoneer does not exempt you from Nepali tax obligations on foreign income. As a general practice, Nepali freelancers earning foreign income are expected to register for a Permanent Account Number (PAN) with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and account for that income at tax time — this is a general description, not a citation to a specific IRD rule we've independently verified, so treat it as a starting point for your own research, not a settled answer. The full picture — including the widely-discussed presumptive tax rate for foreign freelance income, which we have deliberately not stated as settled fact — is on the dedicated tax page. Read it before you assume any amount you receive is fully yours to spend.

Practical tips from how Nepali freelancers actually use it

  • Keep your name consistent. The name on your Payoneer account, your bank account, and your government ID should match — mismatches are the most commonly reported cause of withdrawal friction.
  • Don't wait for your first payment to start verification. Identity verification can take a few business days; start it as soon as you sign up, not when a client is about to pay you.
  • Payoneer is built for frequent, smaller payments. If you're expecting one large, occasional payment from a direct client rather than a stream of freelance-platform payouts, compare it against a direct bank wire — for large one-off amounts, the two rails can come out differently on fees and paperwork.
  • Keep records. Save your Payoneer statements and withdrawal confirmations — you'll want them if you're ever asked to substantiate foreign income for tax purposes.
